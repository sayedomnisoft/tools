Month-end close scorecard: 10 questions for finance heads

For financial controllers and finance managers whose close takes weeks, especially across more than one company. Ten questions, two minutes, and the three things to fix first.

The short answer

Most of a long close is waiting, not working: supplier invoices, stock that does not agree with the books, bank lines nobody owns, and a consolidation rebuilt in Excel.

The fastest gains come from the order of the close and one owner per step, then from making stock post its value as it moves, then from matching intercompany before consolidating.

By Sayed Rashid, ex-auditor, 38 systems taken live. Seven-plus years putting Singapore and regional firms onto one system, and rescuing projects that stalled.

The close, in the order that works

Cut-offDay 1
  • Write down the last invoice, receipt and delivery numbers of the month.

Done when: numbers written down

BankDay 1 to 2
  • Reconcile every account; every unmatched line gets an owner.

Done when: bank agrees to the books

SuppliersDay 2 to 3
  • Accrue goods received but not invoiced, from the goods received list.

Done when: accruals totalled, nobody waiting for invoices

Stock and payrollDay 3 to 4
  • Stock value agrees to the ledger; payroll, CPF and recurring entries posted.

Done when: stock agrees; payroll agrees to the bank

IntercompanyDay 4
  • Match balances between the companies before consolidating.

Done when: both sides agree

Margin and reportsDay 5
  • Margin by product and customer against last month; one line on anything odd.

Done when: reports sent, slowest step noted

The days are an example of the order, not a promise. Start from your own timetable and take one day off at a time.

Questions

How long should a month-end close take?

There is no official standard. A useful target is to take one day off your current close each month until the slowest step is a decision, not a wait.

Why does our month-end close take so long?

Usually because it waits: for supplier invoices for goods already received, for stock to be reconciled to the ledger, for bank lines nobody owns, and for a consolidation done by hand in Excel after everything else.

How do you consolidate several companies faster?

Use one chart of accounts in every entity, match intercompany balances before you consolidate rather than after, and stop rebuilding the consolidation in Excel each month.

What order should the month-end close follow?

Cut-off numbers first, then bank, then suppliers and accruals, then stock and payroll, then intercompany and consolidation, and margin review and reports last, with one owner per step.
