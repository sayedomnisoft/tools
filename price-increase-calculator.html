Percentage increase formula

Price increase % = (new price − old price) ÷ old price × 100. S$40 to S$44 is a 10% increase.

How much to raise prices when costs go up

To keep the same margin %, divide the new cost by (1 − your current margin). To keep the same profit per unit, add the cost increase to the old price. The first protects your margin; the second only protects the dollars.

Worked example: a product costs S$28 and sells for S$40, a 30% margin. The supplier raises the cost to S$31. Keeping a 30% margin means charging 31 ÷ 0.70 = S$44.29, a 10.7% increase. Leaving the price at S$40 cuts the margin to 22.5%. Passing on only the S$3 (S$43) keeps the profit per unit but lowers the margin to 27.9%.

Raising prices without losing customers

  1. Tell trade customers in writing with a date, ideally 30 days ahead.
  2. Explain the reason in one line; most customers accept a cost-driven increase.
  3. Round to clean price points.
  4. Review the whole list, not just the items whose costs you noticed.

Questions

How do I calculate a price increase percentage? Subtract the old price from the new price, divide by the old price and multiply by 100.

How much should I raise prices when costs increase? To keep the same margin, divide the new cost by one minus your margin. A S$31 cost at a 30% margin needs a S$44.29 price.

Is a 10% increase on price the same as a 10% margin? No. Margin is profit divided by price; a price increase is the change in price. Use the margin vs markup guide to avoid mixing them.
