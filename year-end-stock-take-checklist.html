Year-end stock take checklist and count sheet

Count once, and trust the number. This kit is the count plan from two weeks out, the freeze and cut-off note, a count sheet to print, and the way to turn the differences into dollars. Change the square brackets and use it this December.

The short answer

Three rules decide whether you can trust a stock count: the count sheet shows no system quantity, each team is two people and neither keeps the stock records, and nothing moves until sign-off.

Around those rules: fix the date two weeks before, send the freeze note two working days before, write down the cut-off numbers before the first count, and work out the variance in dollars, not units.

Year-end stock take checklist: the count plan

Two weeks beforeFinance, warehouse
  • Fix the date; tell sales, purchasing and the warehouse.
  • Clear the returns shelf: back into stock or written off, with an entry.
  • Post all receipts and deliveries still on paper.
  • List stock held outside.
One week beforeWarehouse lead
  • Tidy and label: one item, one location, one label.
  • Print count sheets by location, with no system quantities.
  • Pair the counters: one counts, one writes.
  • Set a recount limit, such as 2% a line.
On the dayCount teams
  • Freeze movement; write down the cut-off numbers.
  • Count every location and mark each shelf when done.
  • Damaged and expired stock goes on its own sheet.
  • A second pair recounts lines over the limit.
AfterFinance; director
  • Work out the variance in dollars.
  • Write a five-word reason for each difference kept; count the unknowns.
  • Adjust the records once, with one approver.
  • The top three reasons are next year's fixes.

The recount limit of 2% a line is an illustration, not a standard. Pick a limit you will actually enforce.

Fix the date now. Christmas Day (25 December 2026) and New Year's Day (1 January 2027) both fall on a Friday. Official source: Ministry of Manpower, Public holidays, read 11 November 2026.

The freeze and cut-off note

Send it two working days before. What arrives before the freeze is counted and recorded; nothing after it is.

Send

Stock count freeze note

Subject: Stock count on [day, date]: no stock moves from [time] to sign-off

Hi all, we count stock on [day, date]. From [time] until I confirm sign-off: no receiving, no picking, no deliveries, no transfers. Urgent orders are picked before [time] and kept in the area marked "sold, not counted". Goods that arrive during the count stay sealed in the area marked "received after count" and are entered the next working day. Questions to [name].

Thank you, [Your name]

Cut-off numbers

Write these down before the first count. Anything with a higher number belongs to the next period.

  • Last goods receipt number
  • Last delivery order number
  • Last sales invoice number
  • Last transfer or adjustment number

Three rules that decide whether you can trust the number

  1. The count sheet shows no system quantity. A counter who sees 120 on the sheet tends to find 120.
  2. Two people per team, and neither keeps the stock records. One counts, one writes.
  3. Nothing moves until sign-off. Anything that must move goes on a log.

The stock count sheet

There is no system quantity on this sheet, on purpose. Count what is there and write what you counted, in pen. Write the unit you counted in (piece, box of 12, kg): mixed units are a common reason a count looks short. Print one sheet per location.

At the top of each sheet: location or zone, sheet number (of how many), counted by, written by, date and start time.

  • *Note codes: D = damaged, E = expired, N = not ours (customer or supplier stock), S = sold, not yet collected.
  • Cross out mistakes with one line and initial them. Do not use correction fluid.
  • Leave no line blank: write "nil" if the bin is empty.
  • At the bottom: counter (name, signature), writer (name, signature), checked by (finance), time finished.

The system quantity is added later, by someone who did not count, on a separate comparison list. That list, not the count sheet, is where the differences are worked out.

Stock take variance in dollars

A difference in units means little until it has a price. For each line: (counted quantity minus system quantity) × unit cost. Then total the shorts and the overs separately, as well as the net.

The three likeliest causes of a short count

Cause 1

Paperwork and stock out of step

Goods left but the delivery was not recorded, or a receipt was entered before the goods arrived.

The sign: the difference matches one document dated near the count.

First check: the five receipts and five deliveries either side of your cut-off numbers.

Cause 2

Wrong unit or a missed place

Counted in boxes, recorded in pieces. Or stock sits in a second location, at a customer or at the forwarder.

The sign: the difference is a neat multiple (12, 24, 100) or a whole pallet.

First check: recount with the unit written down, and confirm the list of stock held outside.

Cause 3

Stock used without an entry

Samples, replacements, damaged goods thrown away, internal use, production waste.

The sign: small shorts across many fast-moving lines.

First fix: one log for samples, damage and internal use, entered every week.

Check these three before you assume theft. Each one leaves a trail you can follow.

Questions people ask

What should a year-end stock take checklist include?

Four stages. Two weeks before: fix the date, clear the returns shelf, post paperwork and list stock held outside. One week before: label every location, print count sheets with no system quantities and pair the counters. On the day: freeze movement, write down the cut-off numbers and count every location. After: work out the variance in dollars and adjust the records once, with one approver.

Why should the count sheet not show the system quantity?

Because a counter who sees 120 on the sheet tends to find 120. A sheet with no system quantity makes the counter write what is on the shelf. The system quantity is added later, by someone who did not count, on a separate comparison list.

What is a stock take cut-off?

It is the line between this period and the next. Before the first count, write down the last goods receipt, delivery order, sales invoice and transfer or adjustment numbers. Anything with a higher number belongs to the next period.

How do I calculate stock take variance in dollars?

For each line, take the counted quantity minus the system quantity and multiply by the unit cost. Total the shorts and the overs separately, as well as the net. In the example on this page the shorts are S$1,897, the overs S$440 and the net S$1,457, which is 4.5% of the S$32,550 in the records. The stock take variance calculator does it for a whole list.

What are the most common causes of a short stock count?

In my experience, three. Paperwork and stock out of step, such as a delivery that was not recorded. A wrong unit or a missed place, such as boxes counted as pieces or stock held at a forwarder. And stock used without an entry: samples, replacements and damaged goods thrown away. Check these three before you assume theft.

When should I fix the date for a December 2026 stock count?

Now. Christmas Day (25 December 2026) and New Year's Day (1 January 2027) both fall on a Friday, according to the Ministry of Manpower public holidays page read on 11 November 2026. Tell sales, purchasing and the warehouse two weeks before the count, and send the freeze note two working days before.
