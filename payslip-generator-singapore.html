Short answer

A Singapore payslip must show 12 items (MOM): the full names of the employer and the employee, the date of payment, the basic salary, the start and end of the salary period, allowances, other additional payments, deductions, overtime hours, overtime pay, the overtime period if it differs, and the net salary paid. Give it with the pay, or within three working days. A PDF, a printout or a handwritten slip are all accepted.

Payslip format in Singapore: the 12 items

MOM requires itemised payslips for every employee covered by the Employment Act. The format is up to you; the content is not. Here is each item with the example above:

#MOM itemExample
1Full name of employerExample Trading Pte Ltd
2Full name of employeeTan Mei Ling
3Date of payment30 Nov 2026
4Basic salaryS$2,400.00 (for hourly, daily or piece rates, also the rate and the hours, days or pieces)
5Start and end date of salary period1 Nov 2026 to 30 Nov 2026
6AllowancesTransport allowance S$150.00
7Other additional paymentsBonus, rest day pay, public holiday pay
8DeductionsEmployee CPF S$547.00; no-pay leave
9Overtime hours worked10
10Overtime payS$188.81
11Overtime payment period, if different from item 5Only when it differs
12Net salary paid in totalS$2,191.81

Items 9 to 11 can be left out when overtime pay does not apply. If you pay more than once a month, one consolidated payslip can cover all the payments since the last one.

Worked example

Tan Mei Ling, a sales coordinator aged 30, earns a basic salary of S$2,400 with a S$150 transport allowance. In November she works 10 hours of overtime.

  • Overtime: hourly basic rate = 12 × 2,400 ÷ (52 × 44) = S$12.59; × 1.5 × 10 hours = S$188.81 (the free overtime calculator shows the steps).
  • Gross pay: 2,400 + 150 + 188.81 = S$2,738.81.
  • Employee CPF: 20% of S$2,738.81 is S$547.76; the CPF Board says to drop the cents of the employee share, so S$547. The total at 37% is S$1,013.36, rounded to S$1,013, so the employer pays S$466 (CPF Board rates from 1 January 2026, Singapore citizen aged 55 or below).
  • Net salary paid: 2,738.81 − 547 = S$2,191.81.

CPF depends on age, citizenship status and the wage ceilings, so type the amounts from the CPF Board's own contribution calculator rather than working them out by hand. The payslip maker does not calculate CPF for you on purpose.

Common payslip mistakes

  1. One line for "allowances". Show each allowance, fixed or one-off.
  2. Overtime from gross pay. Overtime is worked out from the basic rate only; allowances do not raise it.
  3. Missing the salary period. The payment date alone is not enough.
  4. Leaving out no-pay leave. Every deduction needs its own line.
  5. Late payslips. Give it with the pay, or within three working days.

Questions

What must a payslip include in Singapore? MOM lists 12 items: full name of employer, full name of employee, date of payment, basic salary, start and end date of the salary period, allowances, other additional payments, deductions, overtime hours worked, overtime pay, the overtime payment period if different, and net salary paid.

Who must get an itemised payslip? All employees covered by the Employment Act. Overtime items can be left out when overtime pay does not apply.

When must a payslip be issued? Together with the salary payment. If that is not possible, within three working days of payment. When employment ends, together with the outstanding salary.

Can a payslip be a PDF or handwritten? Yes. MOM accepts soft copies and hard copies, including handwritten payslips.

Is employer CPF shown on the payslip? It is not one of the 12 required items, because it is not deducted from the employee. Many employers show it for information. The employee CPF contribution is a deduction and must be shown.

How long must payslip records be kept? For current employees, the latest two years. For former employees, the last two years of employment, kept for one year after they leave.
