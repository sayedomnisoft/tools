The short answer

A 13-week cash flow forecast is one column a week and a short list of rows: opening balance, plus money in, minus money out, gives closing balance. Put every amount in the week it reaches or leaves the bank. Then find the lowest closing balance and move one date before that week arrives.

Four rules for filling it in

  1. Cash, not profit. Money goes in the week it reaches or leaves the bank, not the week of the sale.
  2. Customer receipts go in the week you expect the money, not the invoice date and not the due date. If a customer usually pays 20 days late, write it 20 days late.
  3. Whole thousands are enough. S$62,400 is 62. This is a planning sheet, not the books.
  4. Start with today's bank balance. Opening, plus money in, minus money out, gives closing.

Which week is tightest? The weeks of 25 Jan and 1 Feb close at minus 10 and minus 9. Salaries and bonus (72), GST (26) and suppliers (42) all leave in one week.

What can move? A supplier takes 24 in the week of 15 Feb. A customer pays 18 before the holiday, not after. Now they close at 32 and 15. Only two dates moved.

The 20-minute Monday routine

  1. Write last Friday's real bank balance under that week.
  2. Tick what came in and went out, and move anything late.
  3. Add what you learnt: a new order, a bill, a promised date.
  4. Find the lowest closing balance. If it is below your floor, pick one move from the list below and make the call today.

Money in, sooner

Invoice on the day of delivery. Ask a large customer for a payment date before the holiday. Take deposits.

Money out, later

Questions people ask

What is a 13-week cash flow forecast?

A planning sheet with one column a week for 13 weeks, about one quarter. Each week starts with the opening balance, adds money in, takes off money out and ends with a closing balance. It works in cash, not profit: money goes in the week it reaches or leaves the bank.

Should customer receipts go in on the invoice date or the due date?

Neither. Put each receipt in the week you expect the money. If a customer usually pays 20 days late, write it 20 days late. A forecast built on due dates shows cash you will not have.

Which Singapore payment dates fall between 7 December 2026 and 7 March 2027?

From the official pages read on 11 November 2026: CPF enforcement dates of 14 December, 14 January and 15 February; foreign worker levy by 17 December, 18 January and 17 February; the GST return and payment for October to December by 31 January, with the GIRO deduction on 15 February; ECI filing by 26 January or 26 February to keep 10 or 8 instalments for December year-ends on GIRO; and property tax by 31 January 2027. The days of the week on this page are my own calendar arithmetic.

When is the GST return for October to December 2026 due?

On 31 January 2027, one month after the accounting period ends, according to the IRAS page on GST filing due dates read on 11 November 2026. With GIRO, the deduction is on 15 February. The page gives no weekend roll-over for filing, and 31 January 2027 is a Sunday by my own calendar arithmetic, so I would plan for Friday 29 January.

What can I move when a week looks tight?

Dates move more easily than amounts, and far more easily when you ask early. Bring money in sooner: invoice on the day of delivery, ask a large customer for a payment date before the holiday, take deposits. Push money out later: ask a supplier for a later date before it is due, or split one large payment across two weeks. Payroll, CPF, levy and tax dates do not move.

How often should I update the forecast?

Once a week, on Monday, for about 20 minutes. Write in last Friday's real bank balance, tick what came in and went out, move anything late, add what you learnt, and find the lowest closing balance. One person owns the sheet.
