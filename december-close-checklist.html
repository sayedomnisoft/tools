Month-end and year-end close checklist: December, closed day by day

The close day by day, the six things December adds, and a timetable with an owner beside every step. Written for the December 2026 close, and usable for any month.

The short answer

A close finishes on time when every step has one owner, one cover and a test that says when it is done. The steps below run from the week before month end to day 5: cut-off numbers, bank, supplier statements and accruals, stock and payroll, then margins and reports.

December adds six things, because other people will read these numbers. In January 2027, day 1 is Monday 4 January, because 1 January is a public holiday (Ministry of Manpower, read 11 November 2026).

Month-end close checklist: the close, day by day

Before month endMon 21 to Thu 31 Dec
  • Remind sales to hand in every delivery note and staff to submit claims.
  • Chase supplier invoices for goods already received.
  • Fix the stock count date and the cut-off time.
  • Send everyone the timetable.

Done when: Each person knows their step and day

Day 1Mon 4 Jan
  • Cut-off: write down December's last invoice, receipt and delivery numbers.
  • Save statements to 31 Dec for every account.

Done when: Numbers written; statements saved

Day 2Tue 5 Jan
  • Reconcile every bank account. Each unmatched line gets an owner.
  • Deliveries made but not invoiced are invoiced or listed.

Done when: Bank agrees to the books; list explained

Day 3Wed 6 Jan
  • Match supplier statements for the ten largest suppliers.
  • List goods received but not invoiced, and accrue them.

Done when: Ten matched; accruals totalled

Day 4Thu 7 Jan
  • Post the stock count differences and review slow items.
  • Post payroll, CPF and the recurring entries: depreciation, prepayments, loan interest.

Done when: Stock agrees to count; payroll to bank

Day 5Fri 8 Jan
  • Review margin by product line against last month and last December.
  • Explain anything odd in one line. Send the reports.

Done when: Reports sent; slowest step noted

Five days is a target, not a rule. If your close takes twelve days today, aim for ten in January. The dates assume the business does not work on Saturday 2 and Sunday 3 January; change them if your day 1 is different.

What your close costs

Close cost for a year = people × hours a day × days per close × 12 × hourly cost.

Example figures
3 people × 4 hours a day × 9 days × 12 closes = 1,296 hours. At S$35 an hour that is S$45,360 a year. The same sum at 5 days: 3 × 4 × 5 × 12 = 720 hours, or S$25,200. The gap is 576 hours and S$20,160 a year.

How a close gets shorter

  1. Every step has one owner and one cover. "Accounts" is not an owner. A name is.
  2. A step is done when its test is met, not when someone says it is nearly there.
  3. Write down the step that waited longest. Next month, start that step one day earlier, or remove what it was waiting for. One step a month is enough.

Year-end close checklist: what December adds to a normal close

A normal month needs the day-by-day list above. December needs six more things, because these are the numbers other people will read: your directors, your bank, your auditor, your tax agent.

A

Cut-off numbers

The last sales invoice, delivery order, goods receipt, credit note and payment voucher of 2026, on one signed sheet. Then check five documents either side of each: is every one in the right year?

B

Stock count tie-in

The count sheets, the cut-off numbers used on the day, and every movement between the count date and 31 Dec if you counted early. The count total must agree to the stock figure in the books, with each difference explained.

C

Accruals

Costs of 2026 not yet invoiced: bonus and unused leave, commissions, utilities, freight, professional fees, goods received but not invoiced. One line each, with how the amount was worked out.

D

Supplier statements

Ask every major supplier for a statement at 31 Dec. Match each one to your records and list the differences: invoices in transit, payments in transit, disputes.

E

Fixed assets list

Additions with their invoices. Disposals taken off. Items no longer in use marked. Walk the floor once and tick each large item: is it still there, and still used?

F

What your auditor or tax agent asks for first

From experience: the trial balance. Bank statements and reconciliations at 31 Dec. Aged customer and supplier lists that agree to the books. Count sheets and the final stock list. The fixed assets list. A payroll summary that agrees to CPF. Loan statements. The accruals list. Put them in one folder before anyone asks.

What a late December close holds up in Singapore

Four dated things wait on December's numbers. Each is taken from the official page named beside it, read on 11 November 2026. The days of the week are my own calendar arithmetic. Your tax agent decides what applies to your company.

Thu 14 Jan

CPF for December wages is due on 31 Dec. Enforcement applies to employers who have not paid by the 14th of the following month.

Official source: CPF Board, Paying your employees' CPF contributions on time (cpf.gov.sg), read 11 November 2026.

Tue 26 Jan

A company files its ECI within 3 months of its year end, unless the waiver applies. With a December year-end and GIRO, it gets 10 instalments if it files by 26 Jan, 8 by 26 Feb and 6 by 26 Mar.

Official source: IRAS, Estimated Chargeable Income (ECI) filing (iras.gov.sg), read 11 November 2026.

Sun 31 Jan

The GST return and payment for October to December are due. The page states no weekend roll-over for filing.

Official source: IRAS, GST filing due dates and requests for extension (iras.gov.sg), read 11 November 2026.

Mon 1 Mar

Employers in the Auto-Inclusion Scheme submit employment income records by 1 Mar each year.

Official source: IRAS, Submit employment income records (iras.gov.sg), read 11 November 2026.

Questions people ask

What is on a month-end close checklist?

Before month end: delivery notes, staff claims and supplier invoices chased. Day 1: cut-off numbers and bank statements. Day 2: bank reconciliations and deliveries not yet invoiced. Day 3: supplier statements and accruals. Day 4: stock differences, payroll and recurring entries. Day 5: margin review and reports. Each step has one owner, one cover and a test that says when it is done.

How long should a month-end close take?

Five days is a target, not a rule. If your close takes twelve days today, aim for ten next month. Write down the step that waited longest, then start that step one day earlier or remove what it was waiting for. One step a month is enough.

What does a year-end close add to a normal month-end close?

Six things: cut-off numbers on one signed sheet, the stock count tied to the books, accruals for costs not yet invoiced, supplier statements matched at 31 December, an updated fixed assets list, and one folder with what your auditor or tax agent asks for first.

How much does a slow close cost?

People, times hours a day, times days per close, times 12, times hourly cost. With example figures, 3 people at 4 hours a day for 9 days is 1,296 hours a year, or S$45,360 at S$35 an hour. At 5 days it is 720 hours, or S$25,200. The gap is S$20,160 a year. Work out your own figure.

What does a late December close hold up in Singapore?

Four dated things, each taken from its official page on 11 November 2026: CPF for December wages (due 31 December, with enforcement after the 14th of the following month), the corporate tax estimate or ECI (file by 26 January to keep 10 instalments, for a December year-end on GIRO), the GST return and payment for October to December (31 January), and employment income records for employers in the Auto-Inclusion Scheme (1 March).

What will my auditor or tax agent ask for first?

From experience: the trial balance, bank statements and reconciliations at 31 December, aged customer and supplier lists that agree to the books, count sheets and the final stock list, the fixed assets list, a payroll summary that agrees to CPF, loan statements and the accruals list.
