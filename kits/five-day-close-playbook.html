Most of a long close is waiting, not working

As an auditor I saw the same close in every company that took three weeks: the work itself was a few days, and the rest was waiting. Waiting for supplier invoices for goods already received, for a stock figure that agrees with the books, for the one person who knows the accrual.

The order that works is the same almost everywhere: cut-off, bank, suppliers and accruals, stock and payroll, intercompany, then margin and reports, with one owner per step. The days below are an example of the order, not a promise for every business. (Month-end close scorecard)

One rule runs through every prompt: you bring the real figures, AI sorts, checks and drafts, and a person signs off.

What a slow close costs

A worked example from our calculator: a close that takes 12 working days and pulls in 3 people for about half of each day costs about S$40,926 a year in salary at S$4,500 a month. The same close in 3 days costs about S$10,232. Put your own numbers in. (Month-end close cost calculator)

Four habits get most small businesses to five working days: post transactions as they happen, reconcile the bank weekly, lock the period once it is closed, and build the reports once. (source)

Ground rules for every promptUse a business account with data training switched off, or remove names first. Never paste NRIC numbers, salaries by name, bank account numbers or passwords. AI can miscount and can sound sure when it is wrong: check every figure against your own records before anyone acts on it. This is general guidance from experience, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Cut-off and Bank Team

Team 1 of 4 · 3 prompts

Goal, day 1 to 2: the last invoice, receipt and delivery numbers of the month written down, and every bank account reconciled with an owner for each unmatched line. (source)

The cut-off sheet

Act as a month-end close coordinator. Month being closed: [month and year]. Our documents: [sales invoices, delivery orders, goods received notes, purchase invoices, credit notes, and how each is numbered] Build a one-page cut-off sheet: 1. A row for each document type: last number issued in the month, date, who confirms it, and where the next number starts. 2. Three checks for goods that moved around month-end: delivered but not invoiced, invoiced but not delivered, received but no supplier invoice yet. 3. A sign-off line for the person who confirms cut-off on working day 1. Keep it to one page that can be reused every month.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
your document types and numbering. No figures needed.
Good output
a sheet filled in on the first morning. It settles "which month does this belong to" before anyone argues.

Bank reconciliation triage

I will paste two lists for one bank account. List A: bank statement lines for [month] (date, description, amount). List B: cash book or ledger lines for the same account (date, reference, amount). [paste List A] [paste List B] 1. Match lines that agree on amount and are within 5 days of each other. 2. List everything unmatched on each side, largest first. 3. For each unmatched line, give the most likely reason (timing, bank fee, duplicate, missing entry) and the role who would know. 4. Show the remaining difference and check it equals the sum of the unmatched lines. Do not invent entries. If something is unclear, say so.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
exports from the bank and from your accounting system.
Good output
a short list of unmatched lines, each with an owner, and a difference that ties.

Move the bank to weekly

Our bank reconciliation is done once a month and takes [hours]. We have [number] accounts and about [number] lines a month. Design a weekly routine that takes 30 minutes or less: 1. Which day and who, with a named backup role. 2. The exact steps, in order. 3. What gets escalated the same day and to whom. 4. How the month-end reconciliation changes once the weekly one exists. Plain steps a new hire could follow.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the number of accounts, lines and who does it today.
Good output
a routine short enough to survive a busy week. Weekly bank is the habit that shortens day 1 the most.

Suppliers Team

Team 2 of 4 · 3 prompts

Goal, day 2 to 3: goods received but not invoiced are accrued from the goods received list, and the main supplier statements agree with your ledger. (source)

Accrual list from goods received

I will paste two lists for [month]. List A: goods received in the month (date, supplier, order number, item, quantity, order price). List B: supplier invoices already entered (supplier, invoice number, date, order number, amount). [paste List A] [paste List B] 1. Match receipts to invoices by order number, then by supplier and amount. 2. List receipts with no invoice yet: supplier, order, value at order price. 3. Total the list by supplier. This is the accrual to review. 4. List invoices with no receipt, for someone to check before they are paid. Do not guess prices that are not in the lists.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
your goods received list and purchase invoice listing.
Good output
an accrual built from what arrived, not from memory. It removes the wait for late invoices.

Supplier statement against our ledger

Supplier statement for [supplier, or "Supplier A"] as at [date]: [paste lines: date, their invoice number, amount, balance] Our ledger for the same supplier: [paste lines: date, invoice number, amount, paid or unpaid] 1. Match by invoice number, then amount. 2. List items on their statement that are not in our ledger, and items in our ledger that are not on theirs. 3. List payments we made that they have not shown. 4. Give both balances and a reconciliation that explains the difference line by line. Do not invent invoices.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the statement and your ledger for that supplier.
Good output
a reconciliation you could send to the supplier the same day.

Chase the missing invoices, politely

Write a short email to a supplier asking for invoices for goods already received. Details: [order numbers, delivery dates, items] Tone: friendly and specific. Ask for the invoices by [date] and say where to send them. Give me two versions: a first request, and a reminder for one week later. No threats, no jargon, under 90 words each.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the order and delivery details.
Good output
two emails ready to send. Asking on the day goods arrive is what ends the month-end wait.

Stock and Payroll Team

Team 3 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal, day 3 to 4: the stock value agrees with the ledger, payroll is posted, and intercompany balances agree on both sides. (source)

Tie stock to the ledger

Stock valuation report as at [month-end]: [paste by category or location: quantity, value] Ledger balance for inventory at the same date: [paste] Movements we know were late or adjusted: [paste] 1. Show the difference between the stock report and the ledger. 2. List the most likely causes in order (receipts not posted, deliveries not posted, cost changes, manual journals, negative quantities) and what evidence would confirm each. 3. Write the 5 checks to run, with the report or screen for each, without naming a software product. 4. Say what should be corrected in the stock records and what in the ledger. Use only the figures given.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the valuation summary and the ledger balance.
Good output
a short list of checks in order. When stock and ledger agree every month, the audit gets short too.

Intercompany mismatch finder

We have [number] companies. For [month], here is what each says it owes or is owed by the others: [paste for each company: counterparty, receivable, payable, sales to, purchases from] 1. Build a matrix and mark every pair that does not agree. 2. For each mismatch, give the amount and the most likely reason (timing, exchange rate, an invoice entered on one side only). 3. Write the one-line question to send to each finance contact. 4. Suggest a monthly cut-off rule for intercompany invoices that would prevent most of these. Do not net off amounts without showing them.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the intercompany balances from each company.
Good output
the pairs that disagree, found on day 4 instead of at consolidation.

Reports Team

Team 4 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal, day 5: margin checked, reports built once, and one step chosen to make next month a day faster. (source)

Margin sense check before the pack goes out

This month and the last three months, by product group or customer group: [paste revenue, cost of sales, gross margin] Known events: [price changes, a large order, stock adjustments, exchange rate moves] 1. Flag any margin that moved more than 2 points and say which line drives it. 2. For each flag, give the most likely cause from the events and the one document that would prove it. 3. List anything that looks like an error, not a business change (cost of sales missing, a credit note in the wrong month). 4. Write 5 bullet points of commentary for the directors in plain English. No guessing beyond the figures given.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
summary lines, not the whole ledger.
Good output
errors caught before the pack goes out, and commentary a director reads in a minute.

Take one day off next month

Here is how our close ran this month: [paste each step, owner, working day started, working day finished, and what it waited for] 1. Show the total working days and how many were waiting, not working. 2. Rank the waits by days lost. 3. For the biggest wait, propose one change we can make this month (who, what, by when) and how we will know it worked. 4. Rewrite next month's timetable with that one change. One change only. Small and certain beats large and hopeful.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
an honest log of the close just finished.
Good output
next month's timetable, one day shorter. Repeat monthly until you are at five.

When the spreadsheets stop scaling

These prompts take days off a close. They do not fix the root cause I see most often: the month's data was never captured properly while the month was happening, so finance spends the close rebuilding it. If sales, stock and purchases live in different files, the close will always be a reconstruction.
