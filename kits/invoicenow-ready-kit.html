This is not a filing change. It is a visibility change

Every GST-registered business in Singapore has to send invoice data to IRAS through InvoiceNow, phased in by size and finishing April 2031. Most owners I meet treat it as a software question. It is mostly a habits question.

After your date, IRAS also receives the invoice-level data behind each GST return. Tax codes applied by habit, invoices raised in a Word template for the odd job, credit notes handled loosely, the same customer saved as two records: each of these becomes visible. (When your business must join)

So this kit works in the order that keeps it calm: find your date, clean the data, close the gaps in how you invoice, then ask your software provider the right questions.

Your date depends on one number

For businesses already GST-registered, the phase is fixed by total annual supplies for 2025: Box 4 of the GST returns, added across the periods ending in 2025, including zero-rated and exempt supplies. Existing registrants join on 1 April 2028 (S$200,000 or less), 1 April 2029 (S$1,000,000 or less), 1 April 2030 (S$4,000,000 or less) or 1 April 2031 (above). The smallest go first. (InvoiceNow date calculator, figures checked against IRAS on the page)

Newly registered businesses follow different rules, and IRAS has been notifying businesses registered before 2026 of their individual date. Use the calculator for your own case, and treat the IRAS notice as the final word. (source article, InvoiceNow explained)

Ground rules for every promptUse a business account with data training switched off, or remove names first. Never paste NRIC numbers, salaries by name, bank account numbers or passwords. AI can miscount and can sound sure when it is wrong: check every figure against your own records before anyone acts on it. This is general guidance from experience, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Date Team

Team 1 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal: your implementation date for every GST-registered entity, written down with the figure it rests on. (date calculator)

Add up our Box 4 for 2025

I will paste the Box 4 figure (total supplies) from each GST return whose accounting period ended in calendar year 2025. [paste each period and its Box 4 figure] 1. Add them up and show the working. 2. Remind me what Box 4 includes (standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt supplies) and ask me to confirm nothing was left out, for example exports. 3. State the total clearly so I can enter it in the InvoiceNow date calculator. Do not tell me my date yourself. I will read it from the calculator and from the IRAS notice.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
Box 4 from each 2025 return.
Good output
one total with its working. Exporters are often caught out because zero-rated supplies count.

One table for all our entities

We have [number] entities. For each: [name or "Company A", GST-registered or not and since when, Box 4 total for 2025 if registered, accounting software used] Build a table: entity, GST status, 2025 total supplies, the date from the calculator (I will fill this in), software, and who owns getting it ready. Add a column for "date confirmed by IRAS notice: yes or no". Then list the entities in the order of their dates, earliest first, and say which one sets our timetable.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
one line per entity.
Good output
a single page that shows which entity goes first. Growing or shrinking later does not move the phase.

Data Team

Team 2 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal: one record per customer with the right legal name and identifiers, and tax codes that mean what they say.

Find duplicate customers

Here is our customer list: [paste name, registration number if held, address, last invoice date] 1. Group records that are probably the same legal entity (for example "ABC Trading" and "ABC Trading Pte Ltd", different spellings, old addresses). 2. For each group, propose the record to keep and why. 3. List customers with no registration number on file, most recently invoiced first. 4. Write the short email we can send those customers to confirm their legal name and number. Do not merge anything yourself. This is a list for a person to review.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
an export of your customer master. Company names only, no personal data.
Good output
a merge list for review and a list of customers to contact. Two records for one customer is no longer a cosmetic problem.

Audit our tax codes

Here are our sales invoices for [period]: [paste customer, country, item or service, amount, tax code used] Our tax codes and what each is meant for: [paste] 1. List invoices where the tax code looks inconsistent with the customer's country or the item, and say why it looks odd. 2. Group the oddities by pattern (for example: one user always picks the same code). 3. Write the questions a tax adviser should answer for each pattern. Do not decide the correct GST treatment. Flag only, and say so.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
an invoice listing with codes. Remove customer names if you prefer.
Good output
patterns to take to your tax adviser. Codes applied by habit are the first thing invoice-level data shows.

Process Team

Team 3 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal: every invoice and credit note raised inside the system, by a written rule.

Find the invoices raised outside the system

List every way an invoice or credit note leaves our company today: [describe: accounting system, a Word or Excel template for odd jobs, a point-of-sale system, a web store, marketplaces, handwritten books, a sister company's system] 1. Build a table: channel, who uses it, roughly how many a month, whether it reaches the accounting system automatically, by re-typing, or not at all. 2. Mark every channel that would not send data through InvoiceNow as it stands. 3. For each marked channel, give two options: bring it into the system, or stop using it, with what each needs. 4. Rank them by number of invoices.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
an honest list, including the template "someone uses sometimes".
Good output
the gaps, ranked. The Word template for the occasional odd job is the classic one.

Write our credit note rules

Write a one-page rule for credit notes at a Singapore [type of business]. Today: [describe how credit notes, refunds, discounts after invoice and corrections are handled, and by whom] Cover: when a credit note is required instead of editing or deleting an invoice; what it must refer to; who approves it; how soon it is issued; how a wrong customer or wrong amount is corrected. Plain steps, no legal language. End with 3 examples from our business showing the right handling. Mark anything where the tax treatment should be confirmed with our adviser.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
how corrections really happen today.
Good output
a rule staff can follow. Loose credit notes are the second classic gap.

Software Team

Team 4 of 4 · 2 prompts

Goal: know whether InvoiceNow is a settings change or a purchase for you, before you talk to anyone selling. (InvoiceNow software guide)

Questions for our current software provider

We use [accounting or business software, or "an accounting package"] and our InvoiceNow date is [date from the calculator]. Write the email to our provider asking: 1. Are you on the accredited list for InvoiceNow, and for which product and edition? 2. Is sending invoice data to IRAS included in our current plan, or is it an add-on? What does switching it on involve? 3. What must be true of our customer records and tax codes before it is switched on? 4. Can we test with a few invoices before our date? 5. What changes for invoices that start in our point-of-sale, web store or other systems? Polite, specific, under 200 words. Ask for answers in writing.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
your software name and your date.
Good output
a written answer from your provider. If your package is already accredited, this may be a setting, not a purchase.

Our readiness plan on one page

Our InvoiceNow date: [date]. Today: [date]. Open items from the other teams: [paste duplicates to merge, tax code questions, channels outside the system, provider answers] Build a one-page plan working back from 3 months before our date: 1. Tasks in order, each with an owner by role and a finish date. 2. A test step with real invoices before the date. 3. What we will check in the first month after going live. 4. The 3 risks most likely to delay us, and what we do this month about each. No task without an owner.
What to paste in, and what good looks like
Paste in
the outputs of the earlier prompts.
Good output
a plan that finishes three months early. Find out your date first, then choose.

If your software cannot get there

Sometimes the answer from the provider is no, or the invoices live in three places and no setting will join them. That is the moment InvoiceNow becomes a system decision, and it is better made a year early than a month late.
