Payment reminder email templates and a stop-supply rule: the Get-Paid Kit

Late payment often starts before the customer does anything. This kit fixes your own side first, then gives you the reminder rhythm, the wording to paste and a stop-supply rule your sales team can live with. Change the square brackets and use it today.

The short answer

Send three reminders, on day 1, day 7 and day 14 after the due date. Call on day 15 and do not end the call without a date. Put the account on hold at day 21, under a rule the customer was told about when the account was opened. Every message covers one invoice or one total, asks one question and names one date.

Before any of that, check five things on your own side. No reminder can rescue an invoice that never reached the person who pays.

Before you chase: five checks on your own side

Fix these first. Each one is a reason an invoice sits unpaid that has nothing to do with the customer's willingness to pay.

  1. The invoice leaves within 24 hours of the delivery or the job. Every day it waits on your side is a day added to their terms.
  2. It carries their reference. A purchase order number, a contract number or the name of the person who ordered. No reference is the most common reason an invoice sits in an approval queue.
  3. It goes to the person who pays, with the person who ordered in copy. Ask every customer once: "Which address do invoices go to, and when is your payment run?"
  4. The due date is written as a date. "Due 18 December 2026", not "30 days".
  5. Paying is easy. Bank details and your PayNow details are on the invoice, and the customer knows which invoice number to quote.

The 10-minute Monday routine

  1. Open the list of unpaid invoices, oldest first.
  2. Send that day's reminders: anything that reached day 1, 7 or 14 since last Monday.
  3. Write the promised payment date beside every reply. A promise without a date is not a promise.
  4. Tell sales which accounts are on hold before they take the next order.

One person owns this routine, and one person covers when they are on leave.

Payment reminder email templates: day 1, day 7 and day 14

These are email versions. For WhatsApp, use the first two sentences and attach the invoice. Keep every message to one invoice or one total, one question, one date.

Day 1

Reminder 1: assume an oversight

Subject: Invoice [number], [amount], due [date]

Hi [Name], a quick note that invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [date] and we have not seen the payment yet. A copy is attached. If it has been paid, please ignore this and send me the payment reference so I can match it. If something is holding it up on your side, such as a missing PO or a question on the amount, tell me today and I will sort it out.

Thank you, [Your name]

Day 7

Reminder 2: ask for a date

Subject: Invoice [number] is 7 days overdue: payment date?

Hi [Name], invoice [number] for [amount] is now 7 days past its due date of [date]. Your statement is attached; the total overdue is [amount]. Could you reply with the date we can expect payment? If the invoice is waiting for approval, tell me who it is with and I will contact them directly.

Thank you, [Your name]

Day 14

Reminder 3: firm, and fair warning

Subject: Invoice [number], 14 days overdue: please confirm by [day, date]

Hi [Name], invoice [number] for [amount] is 14 days overdue and I do not yet have a payment date. Please confirm by [day, date] when it will be paid. Under our credit terms we pause new orders on accounts more than [21] days overdue, and I would much rather not get there. I will call you tomorrow morning to settle this.

Regards, [Your name]

Statement of account cover note

Send a statement every month, not only when something is late. A customer who sees the same list you see has fewer reasons to be surprised.

Monthly

Statement cover note

Hi [Name], your statement at [date] is attached. Total outstanding is [amount], of which [amount] is overdue. The two oldest are [number] and [number]. Could you check it against your records and tell me about anything you do not recognise? If all is in order, when is your next payment run?

The day 15 call: four questions

  1. Did you receive the invoice, and is it the right one to pay from?
  2. Is anything on it disputed? If yes, what, and how much is not disputed?
  3. Who approves it, and when is your next payment run?
  4. What date shall I write down?

Never end the call without a date. Confirm it in one line by email the same day.

These are reminders, not legal notices. Send them in your own company's name. They make no threats and state no legal position. If a customer disputes an invoice, settle the dispute first and ask them to pay the undisputed part meanwhile.

A stop-supply rule your sales team can live with

Reminders only work if something happens when they are ignored. Put this in your credit terms and tell customers when they open an account, not when they are already late.

Rule

Stop-supply rule for your credit terms

  1. Every credit customer has terms and a credit limit, in writing.
  2. An account with any invoice more than [21] days overdue, or over its limit, goes on hold: no new orders are delivered until it is cleared or a payment plan is agreed in writing.
  3. Only [the director or finance lead] can release a hold, in writing, with the reason.
  4. Sales see the hold list every Monday, before they take orders.
  5. The customer is told before the hold starts (Reminder 3). Never after a delivery has been refused.
Day 21

Hold notice

Hi [Name], as mentioned last week, invoice [number] for [amount] is now more than [21] days overdue, so under our credit terms the account is on hold from today. We will release your next order as soon as the payment is received, or once we have agreed a payment plan in writing. I would like to get this cleared quickly. Can we speak today?

The one number to watch: days to get paid

Days to get paid = amounts customers owe you ÷ credit sales in the period × days in the period.

Example figures
Customers owe S$310,000. Credit sales in the last 90 days were S$600,000. 310,000 ÷ 600,000 × 90 = 46.5 days. On 30-day terms that is 16.5 days late. Cash tied up beyond your terms: S$600,000 ÷ 90 × 16.5 = about S$110,000.

Questions people ask

When should I send the first payment reminder?

On day 1 after the due date. The first reminder is friendly and assumes an oversight: it names the invoice number, the amount and the due date, attaches a copy, and asks for the payment reference if the invoice has already been paid.

How many payment reminders should I send before I stop supplying?

Three, on day 1, day 7 and day 14 after the due date, followed by a phone call on day 15. The third reminder mentions the hold rule, so the customer is told before the account goes on hold at day 21, never after a delivery has been refused.

What is a stop-supply rule?

A written credit term which says that an account with any invoice more than a set number of days overdue (21 in this kit), or over its credit limit, goes on hold: no new orders are delivered until it is cleared or a payment plan is agreed in writing. Only a named person, such as the director or finance lead, can release a hold.

How do I work out days to get paid?

Divide the amounts customers owe you by your credit sales in the period, then multiply by the days in the period. With example figures: S$310,000 owed and S$600,000 of credit sales in the last 90 days gives 46.5 days. On 30-day terms that is 16.5 days late. The DSO calculator does the same sum.

What should a statement of account cover note say?

The statement date, the total outstanding, how much of it is overdue and the two oldest invoice numbers. Then ask the customer to check it against their records, and end with one question: when is your next payment run? Make the statement here.

Are these payment reminders legal notices?

No. They are reminders sent in your own company's name. They make no threats and state no legal position. If a customer disputes an invoice, settle the dispute first and ask them to pay the undisputed part meanwhile. For formal recovery, speak to a lawyer.
