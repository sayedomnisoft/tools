What to put on an invoice

  • The word "Invoice" (or "Tax Invoice" if you charge GST) and a unique invoice number
  • The invoice date and the due date or payment terms
  • Your business name, address and registration number (UEN in Singapore)
  • The customer's name and address, and their PO number if they gave you one
  • Each item or service with quantity, unit price and line amount
  • Subtotal, any discount, tax and the total due
  • How to pay: bank account, PayNow UEN or a payment link

Your customer's accounts team matches each invoice to their purchase order and delivery order before paying, so a missing PO number or a vague description is the most common reason an invoice sits unpaid.

Tax invoices in Singapore

If your business is GST-registered and your customer is GST-registered too, you generally have to issue a tax invoice within 30 days of the supply. It must show the words "Tax Invoice", your name, address and GST registration number, an invoice number and date, the customer's name and address, a description of the goods or services, the amount before GST, the GST rate and amount, and the total including GST. Switch on GST in the generator and it uses the right title and shows the GST lines. Only GST-registered businesses may charge GST, so if you are not registered, leave it off.

IRAS is also moving GST-registered businesses onto InvoiceNow, the national e-invoicing network, in phases. A PDF is still what most customers receive today; here is who has to switch, and when.

Numbering, terms and getting paid

Use one running sequence (INV-0001, INV-0002 and so on) and never reuse a number; the generator suggests the next one after each download. Common terms are 30 days from the invoice date for trade customers and due on receipt for one-off jobs. Put your payment details on every invoice.
