Reorder Point and Safety Stock Calculator

Find the stock level at which to place the next order: demand during the supplier lead time plus safety stock, worked out three ways (service level z-score, max minus average, or days of cover), with the Excel formulas to copy. Free, no signup, nothing leaves your browser.

Short answer

Reorder point = average daily demand × lead time in days + safety stock. Selling 40 units a day with a 7-day lead time uses 280 units while you wait for the delivery. Safety stock at a 95% service level is 1.65 × the standard deviation of daily demand × √lead time: with a standard deviation of 12 that is 1.65 × 12 × √7 = 53 units, so you reorder at 333 units.

In Excel: =AvgDemand*LeadTime + NORM.S.INV(0.95)*STDEV.S(DailySales)*SQRT(LeadTime).

The reorder point formula

Reorder point (ROP) = lead-time demand + safety stock, where lead-time demand = average daily demand × average lead time in days. Use the same unit throughout: if lead time is in weeks, use weekly demand.

Three ways to set safety stock

MethodFormulaUse it when
Service level (z-score)z × σd × √LTYou have daily or weekly sales history and want a stated in-stock rate
z-score with lead-time variabilityz × √(LT × σd² + d² × σLT²)The supplier is as unpredictable as your demand
Max minus average(max daily demand × max lead time) − (average daily demand × average lead time)You have little history but know the worst week you have had
Days of coveraverage daily demand × daysA quick rule for low-value items

z values: 90% = 1.28, 95% = 1.65, 97% = 1.88, 98% = 2.05, 99% = 2.33. A 95% service level means you expect to run out before the delivery arrives in about 1 order cycle in 20.

Worked examples (40 units a day, 7-day lead time)

  • z-score, 95%: standard deviation of daily demand 12. Safety stock = 1.645 × 12 × √7 = 52.2, rounded up to 53. ROP = 280 + 53 = 333 units.
  • With lead-time variability: the lead time also varies by 2 days (σLT = 2). Safety stock = 1.645 × √(7 × 144 + 1,600 × 4) = 142. ROP = 422 units. An unreliable supplier costs more stock than unpredictable customers.
  • Max minus average: the worst day was 60 units and the slowest delivery 10 days. Safety stock = 60 × 10 − 40 × 7 = 320. ROP = 600 units: safe, but it ties up the most cash.

Use at least 8 to 12 weeks of daily sales, leave out days the item was out of stock (they show zero demand that was really lost sales), and recalculate monthly or whenever the supplier changes.

Common mistakes

  1. Counting only stock on hand. Compare the reorder point with stock on hand plus stock already on order, or you will order twice.
  2. Using calendar days for demand and working days for lead time. Pick one.
  3. One service level for everything. Keep 98 to 99% for the items that stop a sale or a job, and 90% for slow, cheap lines.
  4. Setting it once. A reorder point from last year's sales is wrong after a price change or a new customer.

Questions

How do you calculate a reorder point? Multiply average daily demand by the lead time in days, then add safety stock. 40 units a day with a 7-day lead time and 53 units of safety stock gives a reorder point of 333.

What is the safety stock formula with a z-score? Safety stock = z x standard deviation of daily demand x square root of lead time in days. For a 95% service level z is about 1.65.

What z-score should I use for safety stock? 1.28 for 90%, 1.65 for 95%, 1.88 for 97%, 2.05 for 98% and 2.33 for 99%. Higher service levels need much more stock for each extra point.

How do I calculate a reorder point in Excel? Use =AvgDemand * LeadTime + NORM.S.INV(0.95) * STDEV.S(DailySales) * SQRT(LeadTime), with your own cell references, then ROUNDUP to whole units.

What is the difference between safety stock and reorder point? Safety stock is the buffer kept for demand or delivery surprises. The reorder point is the stock level that triggers a new order: the demand expected during the lead time plus that buffer.

Should the reorder point include stock on order? Compare the reorder point with stock on hand plus stock already on order. Comparing it with stock on hand alone leads to duplicate orders.
